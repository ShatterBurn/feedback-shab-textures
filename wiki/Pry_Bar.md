# Pry Bar

A flat bar on a haft for prying: it takes a fitting off a machine, and lifts a nailed, pinned or sealed block off whole.

## Crafting

Shape a **Pry Head** from a plate or an ingot at a bench (the [Smithing Table](Smithing_Table.md) for metal), then put it with a haft and a tool binding in a crafting grid; it is a [hafted tool](Hafted_Tool.md) whose face is a pry head. A harder piece can be set in the head's edge slot. The head's material sets how tough the bar is and how long it lasts.

The head's **length** axis is the lever: a longer bar lifts a heavier fixing but weighs more.

## Using it

Right-click a block.

- **A machine with fittings** ([Crucible](Crucible.md), a furnace, a [Controller](Controller.md), a cable): a sensor on the face you click comes off first. With none there, the last upgrade fitted comes off, such as a [Refractory Lining](Refractory_Lining.md). A Controller keeps a part its card needs. The fitting drops as its item and the bar wears a point.
- **A fastened block**: rails, doors, trapdoors, signs, buttons, pressure plates, ladders, iron bars, chains and glass panes come off whole and drop as the item they are, with no breaking.

## How it works

A fastened block must be lifted against its hardness: the bar needs enough toughness, and a longer bar counts for more. A bar that is not tough enough bends: it wears a point and nothing lifts. A bar that does lift wears by the load it carried, so a hard block costs more wear than a soft one.
