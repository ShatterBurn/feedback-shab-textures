# Bearing

A [Shaft](Shaft.md) with a proper bearing on it. It does exactly what a shaft does, but loses much less power to friction: **0.04 Su per RPM** against a shaft's 0.1.

## Crafting

```
P R P
R S R
P R P
```

P is a Wrought Iron Plate, R is a Wrought Iron Rod and S is a [Shaft](Shaft.md). Craft it at the [Engineering Table](Engineering_Table.md) with a [screwdriver](Hand_Tools.md#screwdriver) in the tool row and 4 screws ([Fasteners](Fasteners.md)). It needs Mechanical Treatise I: Engines, Flywheels and Dynamotors ([Treatise](Treatise.md)).

## Using it

Use it anywhere you'd use a shaft. Friction adds up on long runs and fast lines, which is where it helps. A bearing also supports the shafts beside it: a run of more than 4 plain shafts without one costs double friction ([Shaft](Shaft.md#unsupported-spans)).

Bearings are also an ingredient in most of the shaft-driven workshop machines: the [Wire Drawer](Wire_Drawer.md), [Rolling Mill](Rolling_Mill.md), [Mechanical Press](Mechanical_Press.md), [Drill Press](Drill_Press.md), [Lathe](Lathe.md) and [Grinding Wheel](Grinding_Wheel.md).

> Not to be confused with the [Rotary Bearing](Rotary_Bearing.md), which turns a built piece, or the [joint voxels](Vehicles.md#joints) of a vehicle built in fine space.
